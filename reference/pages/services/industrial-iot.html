What is Industrial IoT?

Industrial IoT (IIoT) connects machines, sensors, and production lines to software that turns their signals into decisions, using connectivity and edge, an IoT platform with dashboards, and AI that predicts failures. WeEvolveIT builds that software and integration layer from Monterrey, on your cloud, and you own the platform.

IIoT vs IoT: what's the difference?
Consumer IoT is smart homes and wearables. Industrial IoT connects factories and infrastructure, where uptime, safety, and integration with PLCs, SCADA, and MES matter. Higher stakes, harder integration, bigger payoff.

Key takeaways

  • Software, not hardware: we connect the machines you already run, vendor-neutral, and the platform stays yours.

  • Connect, monitor, predict: connectivity and edge, platform and dashboards, AI predictive maintenance, one team.

  • Proven delivery: 1,000+ projects in 17 countries over 15+ years, rated 5.0 on Google and Trustindex.

  • Senior engineers in Monterrey: UTC-6 all year, the same clock as Dallas and Chicago from November to March and one hour behind from March to November.

Why IIoT projects stall

Too many IIoT projects stall as pilots. Ours are built to scale plant-wide.

Where IIoT stalls

  • Pilots that never scale: a proof of concept stuck on one line.

  • Data nobody uses: dashboards no operator opens.

  • Vendor lock-in: a closed platform you can't own or move.

  • OT/IT silos: machine data that never reaches your ERP or MES.

FAQ

What teams ask before connecting the floor.

01What is Industrial IoT (IIoT), and is it hardware or software?

Industrial IoT connects machines, sensors, and PLCs to software that turns their data into decisions. We build that software and integration layer on your cloud: platform, dashboards, and AI predictive maintenance. No hardware, no vendor lock-in, and the platform, code, and data are yours.

02What does an industrial IoT consulting engagement include?

IIoT consulting starts with discovery: we map your machines, protocols, and costliest losses, assess OT/IT gaps, and pick what to connect first. You get a costed roadmap to predictive operations, with what to build and in what order. The same team can then build it.

03What are examples of industrial IoT applications?

The ones that pay off first: predictive maintenance on motors, bearings, and pumps; real-time OEE monitoring; asset tracking; energy and condition monitoring; quality control; and remote monitoring of hazardous sites. They apply across manufacturing, energy, logistics and cold chain, and heavy industry.

04Do you integrate with my ERP, MES, or SCADA?

Yes. IIoT only pays off when data reaches decisions. We wire machine data into your ERP, MES, SCADA, and historian over MQTT, OPC-UA, or Modbus, and re-architect legacy setups so on-prem PLCs feed cloud analytics and the floor and office share one source of truth.

05How does IoT predictive maintenance work?

Sensors stream vibration, temperature, and current data to the platform, where anomaly detection and machine-learning models spot a failing motor, bearing, or pump before it stops the line. You fix it on a schedule, not in an emergency, and cut unplanned downtime.

06How much does an industrial IoT project cost?

It depends on how many machines and lines you connect and what you want to predict. We work three ways: a monthly Agentic Operations service, a fixed-scope project costed after discovery, or a dedicated nearshore team from $50 USD per hour. You own everything we build.

07What is Industry 4.0, and how does Industrial IoT fit in?

Industry 4.0 is the shift to connected, data-driven manufacturing, where machines and business systems share data in real time and AI helps decide what to do next. Industrial IoT is the layer that makes it possible, starting with predictive maintenance and OEE monitoring.

08Do you work with plants in Mexico and the US?

Yes. Our HQ is in Monterrey, in Mexico's manufacturing belt, and we serve plants across Mexico and the US. Monterrey is on UTC-6 all year: the same clock as Dallas and Chicago from November to March, and one hour behind them from March to November.
